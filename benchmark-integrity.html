Research/Benchmark Integrity
Attribution Integrity

Maintaining Integrity Across the Benchmark Life Cycle

I study how evaluation results can remain attributable to capability before exposure, recover diagnostic value after exposure, and stay informative as models and training data change.

Why Benchmark Integrity Degrades

Once benchmark content or benchmark-specific patterns enter training and optimization pipelines, high performance may reflect familiarity rather than the capability the benchmark was designed to measure. Integrity therefore requires more than constructing a strong benchmark once; it requires managing that benchmark over time.

The Benchmark Integrity Life Cycle

The four stages address different points at which exposure and repeated use can weaken the interpretation of a score.

01

Preserve

Evaluate black-box models while keeping protected benchmark data private.

02

Diagnose

Identify when exposure has changed behavior enough to overstate capability.

03

Repair

Remove exposure-driven advantages while preserving the capability under test.

04

Maintain

Adapt evaluations as models evolve and diagnostic value erodes through use.

Research Program

Evaluation Without Exposure

The Private Evaluation Protocol estimates performance from limited black-box interaction while keeping the private remainder hidden. Current work studies informative interactions, behavioral agreement, and transfer across nearby benchmarks.

Diagnosing Exposure

I study modality-specific behavioral signatures of exposure and methods for distinguishing benchmark familiarity from genuine generalization.

Repairing Compromised Evaluations

I investigate whether meaning-preserving transformations can remove exposure-driven advantages without changing the capability being evaluated.

Continuous Maintenance

I study how evaluation suites can be refreshed and monitored as models, training corpora, and benchmark-specific optimization practices evolve.

Open Research Questions
