Teaching Philosophy

My central goal as an educator is to help students develop intellectual independence. Students should leave a course able to learn on their own, adapt what they know to unfamiliar situations, and approach difficult problems with curiosity rather than fear. I consider understanding genuine when a student can explain a concept in their own words, create an analogy they were not given, and apply the idea in a setting the course did not cover.

I begin with problems that make a concept necessary, develop the theory needed to solve them, and then return to the original problem to examine why the solution works and where it may fail. I also give students room to revise unsuccessful solutions and gradually remove scaffolding as their confidence grows. Generative AI can support this process, but conceptual decisions, verification, and explanation must remain the student's responsibility. My goal is calibrated trust and a classroom in which students learn to question evidence—including AI output, research papers, and instructors—with curiosity and judgment.
