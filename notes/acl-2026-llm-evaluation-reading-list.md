# ACL 2026 and the Future of LLM Evaluation

Personal reading tracker for the planned blog post:

> **What ACL 2026 Says About the Future of LLM Evaluation**

Last assembled: October 3, 2026

## Scope

This list covers work from the ACL 2026 research ecosystem that contributes to, audits, criticizes, or materially redesigns LLM evaluation. It includes:

- ACL 2026 long and short papers
- ACL 2026 Findings
- ACL 2026 Industry Track
- EvalEval 2026
- GEM 2026
- SemEval 2026 task-overview papers
- RetroEval 2026

It does **not** attempt to include every paper that merely evaluates a model or introduces a domain dataset. SemEval participant system-description papers are also excluded initially; the task-overview papers provide the better field-level view.

## How to use this file

- Change `[ ]` to `[x]` after reading a paper.
- Use `Priority: Core` for the initial blog corpus and `Priority: Extended` for supporting evidence.
- Add short notes directly below each paper using the supplied fields.
- Use the synthesis matrix near the end after finishing each theme.

Suggested note labels:

- **Claim:** Main argument or contribution.
- **Evidence:** What experiment or analysis supports it?
- **Limitation:** What remains unproven or fragile?
- **Trend:** What does it imply about the future of evaluation?
- **Use:** Where might it appear in the blog?

## Candidate thesis statements

Revise these only after reading the Core set.

1. ACL 2026 marks a transition from treating evaluation as score production to treating it as measurement under explicit validity assumptions.
2. The field is replacing static, answer-based tests with dynamic, interactive, and execution-grounded assessments—but has not yet solved how to validate the evaluators themselves.
3. LLM-as-a-judge is becoming evaluation infrastructure, while ACL 2026 simultaneously demonstrates that this infrastructure is prompt-sensitive, biased, domain-dependent, and vulnerable to proxy gaming.
4. Benchmark scores are increasingly understood as perishable claims whose meaning changes with model exposure, data evolution, evaluator choice, and deployment context.
